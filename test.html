The first 90 days of paid marketing: what results are realistic

 

Hi {{ contact.FIRSTNAME }},

You launched ads on a Monday. By the following Friday, you were asking why the pipeline was still empty.

That's one of the most expensive mistakes SaaS founders make with paid. They treat it like a vending machine: put in the budget, expect a demo to drop out.

Paid isn't a lottery ticket. It's a system, and it takes about 90 days to build one that delivers predictable results.

Here's what those 90 days look like when they're run properly.

Days 1–30

Foundation & learning

This month is about data, not demos. Tracking gets wired end-to-end (ad click → signup → CRM), campaigns launch across 2–3 tightly defined ICP segments, and the platforms' algorithms exit their learning phase.

Realistic result: clean conversion data, a clear picture of which keywords and audiences engage, and a handful of early leads. CPLs will be high. That's expected.

Days 31–60

Optimise & cut the waste

Now the data does the talking. Losing keywords and audiences get paused, budget shifts to what converts, and landing pages and ad creative are A/B tested. Retargeting kicks in for visitors who didn't convert first time.

Realistic result: CPL starts dropping, lead quality improves, and the first sales-qualified conversations show up in the pipeline.

Days 61–90

Scale what works

With proven segments and messaging, budget scales into the winners. Lead scoring and sales handoff get tightened so marketing-qualified leads actually become meetings.

Realistic result: a stable, predictable CPL, a repeatable flow of demos, and the numbers you need to forecast pipeline from spend.

What this means for you

If you judge paid in week one, you'll kill campaigns right before they start working. Judge it on the right metric for each phase:

✓Month 1: is tracking clean and is the right audience engaging?
✓Month 2: is cost per lead trending down?
✓Month 3: is pipeline predictable enough to scale?
